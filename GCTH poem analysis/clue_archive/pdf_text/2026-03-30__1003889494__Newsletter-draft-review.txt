Inside the Mind of One of the 
Biggest Junior Mining Investors 
 
Featuring: Michael Gentile  |  March 2026 
 
Michael Gentile spent 18 years as an institutional money manager overseeing close to a billion dollars in small and 
mid-cap equities. In 2018, convinced that gold was entering a major upcycle, he left to invest his own personal 
capital full-time in TSX Venture-listed junior mining companies. He also co-founded Bastion Asset Management 
in Montreal. When his name appears on a TSX Venture press release, that is his personal capital, not Bastion’s, 
committed with a 5 to 10 year horizon. 
Today he is the largest individual shareholder in approximately 30 to 35 junior mining companies simultaneously. 
He does not trade. He does not chase drill results. His exit mechanisms are M&A takeouts and companies that go 
all the way to production. He is, structurally, a private equity investor operating inside a publicly-listed vehicle. In 
this conversation he laid out his complete framework, named his top holdings, pitched an anonymous Argentina 
gold play, and delivered one of the most honest loss stories we have heard. This report is the full breakdown. 
The Framework 
“You can make a good discovery in the mining space and still not make any money due to 
bad capital structure and bad financing decisions.” 
 
This is the sentence that unlocks everything else Gentile said. He describes the venture mining space as the wild 
west of retail speculation. His response was to bring an institutional, professional approach to it: taking large 
stakes, getting close to management, and applying the same systematic thinking he used managing hundreds of 
millions of dollars to micro-cap junior names. 
▪  Management alignment. 
If he does not have a good working relationship with management, he is not investing. He wants a team that 
understands it is a partnership and treats him as a valued cornerstone strategic investor with a seat at the 
table. 
▪  Genuine mine potential. 
He does not trade drill bit stories or promotes. He is only interested in projects that have the potential to go 
all the way to become a mine. His only liquidity events are takeouts or the rare junior that actually makes it to 
production. 
▪  Capital structure discipline. 
Tight share counts and meaningful insider ownership, roughly 20% as a benchmark. Bad financing decisions 
can erase even the best discovery. 
▪  Jurisdictional awareness. 
He applies hard scrutiny to political and social risk every time. His Lucky Minerals Ecuador loss is the case 
study: social unrest, 18 months of locked-out drilling, wrong drill orientation, and bankruptcy. The filter is 
not binary. It is a risk calculation. 
 


On portfolio construction: if 8 out of 10 junior mining investments go to zero over a full cycle, the only way to 
generate strong returns is to guarantee exposure to the 1 to 2 names that go to 20 to 50x. You cannot know in 
advance which those will be. The diversification is not a hedge against bad judgment. It is the strategy. A single 
junior mining stock is not a strategy. It is a lottery ticket. 
The Companies 
 
North Isle Copper and Gold   TSXV: NCX   Most momentum pick 
“The deal with the most momentum right now” 
In at a $15 million market cap, now approaching $1 billion. What separates North Isle from most junior mining 
companies is that it actually has the team, the asset, the government and First Nations backing, and the financial 
firepower to go all the way. Two mining hall of famers on the board. Kevin O’Kane built the last mine on North 
Vancouver Island. When the majors know you can raise the money, build the mine, and get the permitting done, 
it creates urgency for them to act. That is what turns an exploration story into a negotiation. He put another million 
dollars into the recent $115 million financing and still sees a tremendous amount of upside. 
Silver Crown Royalties   CBOE: SCRI   Nobody knows this yet 
“The deal nobody knows and that’s exactly why I love it” 
Approximately 50% of global silver production is a by-product of mining other metals. Those producers do not 
track it, do not disclose it, and do not manage their business around it. The major royalty companies will not pursue 
it because the individual deal sizes are too small for their mandates. Silver Crown CEO Peter Bures built his entire 
company around that gap, acquiring by-product silver royalties at 25%+ IRRs with no competition. Gentile draws 
a direct line to Franco-Nevada in the 1980s: going into a niche nobody else is paying attention to and stacking 
deals one by one into something very large. Franco-Nevada built a $30 billion company from that playbook. 
Astra Exploration   TSXV: ASTR   Gentile owns ~17% 
“About a $60 million market cap. In Argentina. In a prolific gold belt.” 
The La Manchuria project sits in the Deseado Massif in Santa Cruz, Argentina, the same district that hosts 
Newmont’s Cerro Negro (10 million ounces) and AngloGold Ashanti’s Cerro Vanguardia. The asset was held for 
30 years by a private family who never wanted to sell. Astra’s lead geologist spent 20 years convinced the prior 
operators had misread the geology. Phase I drilling in 2025 proved him right: the vein system is far larger than 
previously mapped, open in all directions, with veins beginning to converge at depth. That convergence is the prize. 
Phase II drilling, fully funded, commenced October 2025. Binary outcome: if the convergence theory holds, the re-
rating is dramatic. 
Karus Gold   Pre-public / TBD   Quiet shareholder 
“$32 million market cap, $7 million in cash” 
A BC gold story Gentile holds quietly: roughly one million ounces in the Cariboo Trend at 0.8 grams per tonne, 
with a high-grade secondary asset on Vancouver Island at 21 metres of 23 grams per tonne. Pre-public at time of 
recording, anchored by a commitment from Peter Barone. 
K92 Mining   TSX: KNT   The benchmark compounder 
“I owned it from 45 cents. It’s a $30 stock today.” 
Not a current idea. A benchmark. Held from $0.45 through its entire journey from obscure Papua New Guinea 
explorer to a $7 billion TSX-listed gold producer. He was illiquid the entire way. This is what his model looks like 
when everything goes right. 


What This Means for You 
 
Gentile’s model is not directly replicable for most investors. But the principles behind it are entirely portable. 
▪  The basket is not optional. 
A single junior mining position, no matter how well-researched, is a lottery ticket. The expected value of any 
single junior mining investment, in isolation, is negative because the base rate of failure is simply too high. 
Build a diversified basket of names. You do not need 30 to 35. But you need more than one or two. 
▪  Follow the disclosures as a filter, not a buy signal. 
When Gentile discloses a significant ownership stake, it means the company passed his checklist. That is a 
meaningful signal. It does not mean you should buy immediately and without research. Use his disclosures as 
a research starting list, not a buy list. 
▪  Capital structure matters. 
Pay close attention to the share count, the cash on hand, the warrant overhang, and the history of prior 
financings. A company that has repeatedly raised money at lower and lower prices has already given away a 
significant portion of the potential upside. Gentile made this point explicitly: you can make a good discovery 
and still lose all your money because of bad capital structure decisions. 
▪  The losing positions teach you more than the winners. 
Gentile’s Lucky Minerals Ecuador loss is more instructive than his K92 win because it contains lessons about 
jurisdiction risk, cash runway management, and drill program design that can be applied to future decisions. 
When you lose money in this space, diagnose the loss honestly. Build your own checklist from your own 
mistakes. 
▪  Time is your actual edge. 
This is the one structural advantage retail investors have over institutional capital. A pension fund facing 
quarterly redemption pressure cannot hold a junior mining position for years while the company works 
through permitting and financing cycles. You can. If you can hold with conviction for 5 to 10 years, you are 
competing on ground where institutional money is structurally absent. 
▪  Opportunity exists in the overlooked corners. 
Gentile explained that major royalty companies operate at 5% cost of capital buying 7% IRR royalties. Silver 
Crown gets 25% IRR on deals in a niche nobody else is focused on. Opportunity exists in micro-caps, pre-
public situations, and overlooked niches that have not yet attracted mainstream attention.
