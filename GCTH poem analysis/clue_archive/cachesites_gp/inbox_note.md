# GCTH Grand Prize: found bonus-cache sites and live-chapter leads vs stanzas 8, 9, 12, 13 (desk check, 17 Nov 2026)

**Question (Max):** is the Grand Prize at or near one of the found bonus-cache sites, or near a live chapter's best lead, so that the caches and the poem's stanzas connect?

**Answer: no credible site.** None of the 13 places passes the two tests that set the Grand Prize description apart:
- **Iron** ("where iron sings", "forged"): an iron or magnetite occurrence within 2 km.
- **A standing marker by the shore** ("one marker stands, by shore and pine", FR "une borne se dresse"): within 200 m of calm water.

Only one place has an iron occurrence within 2 km, and that place is a guess: Parc Obalski at Chibougamau. The winners never published the Chibougamau find site, and the iron record there is a copper occurrence with magnetite noted. **No place has a standing marker within 200 m of calm water.** Every survey marker near water at these places is flush: a tablet or bolt set in a wall, a bridge, a rock or a manhole. The criteria that many places do meet (a lake, pine, birch, a trail, public land) are common across the Canadian Shield, so they do not tell the places apart.

## Notes by place

- **Cobalt and Britannia (tied first)** score well only on the generic criteria. Cobalt Lake is a contaminated tailings lake, a wry match for "best not disturb the water's state". But the Cobalt camp produced silver, cobalt and arsenic: no iron or magnetite occurrence lies within 25 km. The markers near the lake are bolts in the foundations of the testing-lab building, the church and the Fraser House, and in the retaining wall behind the station.
- **Britannia:** Browning Lake in Murrin Provincial Park is a calm, stocked lake 1 km from the lookout, with cedar, a little shore pine and birch. Its two markers are a deep bench mark in a manhole (83C199) and a tablet in a boulder (93C009). Neither stands. There is no iron (Britannia is a copper-zinc mine).
- **Chibougamau** is the only place with an iron occurrence within 2 km. It also has lakes and the "Chemin historique minier" trail. But the point checked is the community's guess (Parc Obalski, R-0018), and the winners withheld the real site (R-1276). The QC forest map shows no thuya (cedar) in 25 sampled stands, and no pine either. The QC geodetic markers lie about 690 m from water.
- **The live leads:**
  - McMahon Point and Star Lake fit "shore and pine" but have no iron within 2 km and no survey marker.
  - Côte-de-Cent-Pieds is an urban park with no lake shore.
  - Dana is aspen parkland by a salt lake, with no cedar and no pine.
- **A separate argument against the idea:** every found site has already been visited and searched closely by its finders and by later hunters. Hiding the Grand Prize there would be an odd design choice. This is reasoning, not data.

## Method

All searches use a 2 km radius around the given point. Run 17 Nov 2026.

- **Points:**
  - S1–S8 are from `setter_habits/find_sites.csv`.
  - S9 is Parc régional Obalski, the community guess in the record (R-0018), geocoded with Nominatim.
  - L1 McMahon Point is 46.5395, −81.1324 (R-1296).
  - L2 Dana Recreation Site is 52.2517, −105.7550 (R-1266).
  - L3 is the centre of Star Lake, 49.7552, −95.2426. Penniac Bay (CGNDB 49.7605, −95.222) is 1.6 km away (R-1251, R-1252).
  - L4 Côte-de-Cent-Pieds is 48.09472, −77.77861 (R-0087).
- **Water:**
  - NRCan CanVec 1:50k WMS (`waterbody_50k` and `watercourse_50k`), with distances measured from rendered pixels using `shoremk_canada/scripts/wmsdist.py`.
  - OSM water polygons and their tags (lake, pond, river or sea) from Overpass, through the maps.mail.ru mirror.
  - Named features from CGNDB geonames.
  - "Calm" means a lake, pond or lagoon. Sea, river and falls do not count.
- **Trees:**
  - Quebec: `ori_pee_close_scale` GetFeatureInfo, read as `gr_ess_desc`.
  - BC: VRI `VEG_COMP_LYR_R1_POLY`, fields `SPECIES_CD_1..6`.
  - Both were sampled on a 25-point grid at 700 m spacing.
  - Everywhere else, the cells use general range knowledge and are marked "(gen.)".
- **Iron:** `shoremk/data/iron.json` (ON and QC) plus `shoremk_canada/iron_canada.json`. Two coverage limits:
  - The NB layer lists Fe as a commodity only, so it cannot flag magnetite.
  - YT lists the main commodity only.
- **Markers:**
  - NRCan `getStations.php` (cbn, pvc, hc and hp3d) over a box of about ±2.4 km.
  - QC RESEAU_GEODESIQUE layers 0, 2, 4, 5 and 6.
  - BC GCM and NSCRS from the downloaded copies in `shoremk_canada/dl/`.
  - OSM `survey_point`, `boundary_stone` and `milestone` tags.
  - The earlier candidate CSVs: `boundary_monuments`, `plaques_canada`, `shoremk` and `shoremk_canada`.
  - Each marker's distance to water was checked against CanVec at 2 m per pixel. The marker type comes from the NRCan station descriptions, saved in `raw/sheets.json`.
- **Trail:** OSM `highway=path|footway|track|bridleway` ways and hiking routes.
- **Public land:** a CPCAD point query plus a 2 km box query. Where CPCAD has nothing, the record or OSM is cited.

## Caveats

- **Position errors** run from 0.2 to 3 km (see `pos_err_m`). Dover, Toronto Island, Kleanza and Chibougamau are area points, not spots.
- **Survey markers:** only national and provincial geodetic networks were searched, plus OSM. Municipal control monuments (Toronto, Sudbury, Val-d'Or), Parks Canada markers and private markers are not covered. A standing marker could exist that these sources miss.
- **CPCAD returned nothing at Whiteshell**, which is a provincial park. That looks like a gap in the service, so public land at L3 rests on the record.
- **Iron coverage:** the "(gen.)" tree cells are inferences, and coverage of iron-poor areas (NB, YT) is thin.
- **OSM POI query:** it timed out for S3, S5, S7 and S8 after retries. Markers there come from NRCan, BC GCM and NSCRS only.
