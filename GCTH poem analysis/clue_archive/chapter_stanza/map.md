# GCTH: chapter ↔ stanza map (desk, 17 Nov 2026)

This is a close reading of the 13 chapter articles (EN and FR) against the 13-stanza Grand Prize poem. It tests Max's hypothesis: the stanzas map to the bonus caches (12 + GP, or 6 + 6), and the GP is whatever stanza is left.

**Line numbers** use the 52-line count: stanza *k*, line *j* = L(4(k−1)+j). L2 = "Old riches whisper", L23 = "The water hums", L29 = "Where iron sings", L33 = "One marker stands", L48 = "And buried speaks what no one knows", L49 = "Not marked by gold".

**Sources.** Chapter text comes from scratchpad/arts/out/text/*_en|_fr.txt. Dawson EN and Nova Scotia EN come from the web posts (clue_archive/web_posts/1003882693.txt and 1003892433.txt). Chibougamau comes from the Nelligan-EN/FR-FINAL raw text. Every quote below was copied from those files. A copy of the corpus is in `corpus/`.

**Confidence scale.** CERTAIN means the poem names the place. HIGH means several specific echoes, or one verbatim echo plus a name. MOD means a specific echo, but other chapters could take it. LOW means a possible echo built from common words. Release order is: 1 Dawson (Sep 2025), 2 Cobalt, 3 Golden Triangle, 4 Argentia, 5 Southern BC, 6 Toronto, 7 Bathurst, 8 Val-d'Or, 9 Saskatchewan, 10 Nova Scotia, 11 Manitoba, 12 Sudbury, 13 Chibougamau (bonus, Sep 2026).

## 2. Chapter-by-chapter evidence (exact quotes)

### 1. Dawson / Klondike (EN web post 1003882693; FR klondike_fr)
- **S1, L2–3** ("Old riches whisper through the past / A mirror hides what veins once bore"): EN "Even now, old-timers say the richest vein lies somewhere near an old span, where the water still whispers of treasure." FR "Même aujourd'hui, les anciens disent que le filon le plus riche se trouve quelque part près d'un vieux pont, où l'eau murmure encore des trésors." Four S1 words appear in one sentence: old, rich(est), vein, whispers. This is the final line, which is the usual place for the clue.
- **S1, L1** ("In a land crowned cold and vast"): "this outpost near the Arctic Circle"; "known as the 'King of the Klondike'" (crowned). LOW on its own, but it agrees with L2–3.
- **S1, L4** ("The verdigris behind the lore" / FR "derrière la légende"): "remains the stuff of legend"; FR "Les personnages … restent légendaires", "les légendes de Big Alex, Mulrooney et Klondike Kate". The copper behind the legend: "reopen the Minto copper-gold-silver mine". LOW-MOD.
- **S5, L19** ("Its gleam is but a siren's light" / FR "la lumière d'une sirène"): "The siren call of Nome, Alaska, drew away thousands" / FR "L'appel sirène de Nome". Also "the Yukon River glitters gold under the midnight sun" (L17 "reflects the sun"). MOD as an echo. It does not map Dawson to S5.
- **S6, L23**: "where the water still whispers of treasure" ≈ "The water hums an ancient code". This echo is already on record.
- **S4 (phoenix)**: "its rebirth as a heritage centre"; FR title "ruine et renaissance"; "By 1899, just three years after the bonanza, the gold rush was already over" (fleeting). The article has no fire and no red. LOW.
- **S9**: "they had staked their ground"; "preserve it as a living monument"; "more than 100 heritage buildings stand as guardians of a past". LOW.
- Note: the subscriber GP clue of 25 Sep 2025 ends "It shall not see the midnight sun". That is a Dawson image used as a negation.

### 2. Cobalt (cobalt_en / cobalt_fr)

## 3. Stanza 4 and stanza 7

**Stanza 4** ("Where fire stitched a northern name / And red seams sparked a fleeting flame / No ember now ignites the track / The phoenix flew and won't look back")
- Best: **Timmins / Porcupine, carried by the Cobalt chapter (MOD).** Three points support it. First, the Cobalt chapter is the only text with "northern" next to a town *naming*, in both languages ("whom the northern city was named after" / "la ville du nord" vs "un nom nordique"). Second, Bathurst lists "Sudbury, Timmins and Flin Flon": the S5, S4 and S6 trio. Third, historically, Timmins was laid out in 1912 after the 1911 Great Porcupine Fire (outside knowledge), which fits a town named out of a fire and rising from it. Cobalt adds "pink stains" (seams) and "the rush faded" (fleeting).
- Runners-up: Southern BC (LOW-MOD: "fire in 1921 forced further rebuilding", "A wash of sparks", "keeps reinventing itself"; but not northern and no seams). Dawson (LOW: "rebirth", the rush over by 1899; no fire in the text). Phoenix BC (no chapter covers it; the Southern BC chapter is Britannia only). Klondike (LOW).
- If the reading is right, S4 names a place with **no cache** (Timmins) while pointing through a chapter that has one (Cobalt). That fits "name and cancel": the phoenix "won't look back".

**Stanza 7** ("The Shield bends low through sleeping trees / Where sunken whispers told of ease / But breaks don't speak nor faults confess / What roots may hold in quiet press")
- Best: **Val-d'Or (LOW-MOD).** Supporting lines: "a rolling sea of spruce and rock that, at first glance, gives nothing away" (≈ breaks don't speak), "rumour" (whispers), "the depths of the Great Depression" (sunken), "Valley of Gold" (bends low). The Cadillac Break as "breaks" / "faults" is outside knowledge.
- Second: **Chibougamau (MOD on the word "Shield" alone)**: "hidden beneath all that Canadian Shield"; "the great mines grew quiet" (sleeping). But Chibougamau is already the "chalco … east" of S6. If S6 L21 = Chibougamau and S6 L22 = Manitoba, then S7 = Val-d'Or is the tidiest fit.
- Unsettled. No chapter has a verbatim S7 echo. S7 has the "cancel" shape (L27 negation), so a weak tie is what a decoy stanza would show anyway.

## 4. Structure tests

The tests use the map in §1 (main stanza per chapter: Dawson 1, Cobalt 4, GT 2, Argentia 3, SBC 3, Toronto 11, Bathurst 10, Val-d'Or 7, Sask 13, NS 3, Manitoba 6, Sudbury 5, Chibougamau 6). Script: `scripts/order_tests.py`. The p-values are permutation tests. The map is desk judgement, so treat the p-values as descriptive.

**(a) 12 bonus + 1 GP = 13, one stanza per chapter. Does not fit.**
- 13 chapters land on only **10 distinct stanzas**. S3 takes 3 coastal chapters (SBC, NS, Argentia). S6 takes 2 (Manitoba, Chibougamau). Each of those has a specific echo, and in the Manitoba case a verbatim FR echo.
- Of the 13 chapters, 6 strongly echo more than one stanza: Dawson (S1, S5, S6), GT (S2, S4, S8, S1), Saskatchewan (S13, S12, S3), Cobalt (S4, S5, S9), Nova Scotia (S3, S1, S9), Sudbury (S5, S13). The writing reuses poem diction across chapters. It is many-to-many, not a bijection. This agrees with the null word-overlap result (p = 0.59).
- The arithmetic is off too. Vaccaro (Boyd video, 15 Oct 2026) says "these **14** treasures": 13 bonus + 1 GP against 13 stanzas (R-1864).
- A forced bijection can be built, because there are 13 chapters and 13 stanzas. But it only fills S8, S9 and S12 by pushing LOW echoes (Argentia → S9, NS → S1, Chibougamau → S7) into them. A forced fill leaves no stanza for the GP, so it cannot be what the setter meant.

**(b) 6 + 6. Does not fit.**
- Release 1–6 (Dawson, Cobalt, GT, Argentia, SBC, Toronto) landing in poem 2–7: **4/6** (Cobalt 4, GT 2, Argentia 3, SBC 3). Dawson goes to S1 and Toronto to S11.
- Release 7–12 (Bathurst, Val-d'Or, Sask, NS, Manitoba, Sudbury) landing in poem 8–13: **2/6** (Bathurst 10, Sask 13). The other four go to the *first* half (NS 3, Val-d'Or 7, Manitoba 6, Sudbury 5).

Under the best-supported map, the stanzas with **no chapter echo above LOW** are:
