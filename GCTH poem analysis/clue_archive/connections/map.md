## 5. How it all fits (plain words, to read aloud)

There are two kinds of clues. The Grand Prize clues (the long poem, two short verses, and two cards read out on video by Rob McEwen and Sean Boyd) describe a place: still water like a mirror, old Shield country with cedar and pine, an old marker standing by the shore, a trail where the grass grows tall, and a box tucked away above the ground, not buried. The thirteen chapter clues were each built to find their own smaller box, and each did it with its own trick: hidden capitals, a book cipher, word pairs, a riddle. The poem names some of those chapters only to say "not here", the way it says "not Bay Street". What joins the two halves is not the scenery. From the very first week, the organisers said the monthly clues would "help sharpen your search". Their only exclusion is one Golden Triangle riddle, and that is still on their website. The last card tells us how: collect the digits "from every chapter, every post" until "two numbers mark the treasure's base". The later chapters do carry digits that did not help find their own boxes (New Brunswick's 1, Nova Scotia's 9, Saskatchewan's 2638, Manitoba's 42, Sudbury's two long strings, and Chibougamau's twelve clocks), so those are probably the pieces. Nobody has yet found the rule that turns them into two numbers, and every way of reading them as map coordinates has come out at chance. So the honest picture is this. We know roughly what kind of place it is, and we know what the setters want us to collect. We do not yet know how the collection becomes a place. One cheap next step is to check the other two websites where TNM said its clues are posted, MINING.COM and CEO.CA. One of those copies already left out a number that the original article printed.
