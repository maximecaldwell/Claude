# GCTH — people named in the published clues

Built 13 Nov 2026 from the English clue texts on the Mac (`~/GCTH/clues/*`: article_EN, subscriber text, EN transcripts, bonus/found/winner posts, Grand Prize files, one-year recap). Nothing was written to the Mac. French texts were not mined separately because they name the same people.

**93 people** in the register: 61 historical (incl. 1 appendix entry: depicted, not named), 1 fictional, 15 living public figures (executives, politicians, organizer staff), 16 private individuals (winners, photo credits), who were **not researched**. **29 portraits downloaded** to `portraits/`. Full data: `named_people.csv`.

Legend: ★ = most useful to the hunt (place names, survey history, or Sudbury/Chibougamau/Val-d'Or geology people). Portrait column: file name, or the reason there is none.

## The most hunt-relevant people

- **Sudbury (Ch.12):** the article names **Albert (Pellew) Salter** (1856 compass anomaly), **Alexander Murray** (GSC), **Alfred Selwyn** (GSC director), **Thomas Flanagan** (1883 CPR blacksmith), **William & Thomas Murray, Henry Abbott, John Laughrin** (Murray Mine), **Thomas Frood, James Stobie**, **Samuel J. Ritchie** (Canadian Copper Co.), **Thomas Edison** (Falconbridge 1901), **Thayer Lindsley**, **Edmund Horne**. **Sir William Logan is NOT named** in any clue text, and the article says "Albert Salter", not "A.P. Salter". Mine and township names that are also surnames (Frood, Stobie, Creighton, Murray, Falconbridge) are possible map anchors.
- **Chibougamau (Ch.13):** James Richardson (GSC, 1870), fur trader Peter McKenzie (1903), and inspector Joseph Obalski (1904). Obalski also translated the 1911/12 Chibougamau Mining Commission report into French. A photo from that report, *Prof. Gwillim and group*, shows up in the **Val-d'Or** video (see the appendix entry for J.C. Gwillim: shown in the photo, not named in the text).
- **Val-d'Or (Ch.8):** Jean-Jacques "Jack" Sullivan is the only person named. **Manitoba (Ch.11):** Tom Creighton, plus the fictional Flintabbatey Flonatin.
- **Grand Prize:** Sean Boyd (Agnico Eagle chair) gave the launch quote and presented Clue 5. Rob McEwen delivered Clue 4. Anthony Vaccaro (TNM Group president) hosted.
- **Oddities worth noting:** NB article presents "Murray Brook" (a Bathurst-camp deposit name) as a prospector. Cobalt spells "Willett Green Miller" (correct: Willet). The Golden Triangle winners are spelled "Schwartz" in the posts and "Schwarz" in the recap. Cobalt preserves the garble "near – Long Lake James McKinley". The Chibougamau winner names come from auto-captions and are unreliable.
