Copper Industry

Although native use of copper had been recorded by Champlain, it was not until the latter part of the 19th century that copper deposits were worked on a large scale in Canada. Important mines were opened and smelters were erected in the 1880's and 1890's in the Sudbury area and in southern British Columbia. The discovery of copper ore in north-western Québec in the early 1920's led to the founding of Rouyn-Noranda and the opening of this region to development. Thereafter Canada became one of the World's most important extractors of copper, with British Columbia, Québec and Manitoba leading in production.

Existing plaque: Asbestos, Quebec

Asbestos Industry

Mining of asbestos on a commercial scale began in 1878 but high costs and a limited and unstable demand kept operations small prior to 1900. Before 1949, Canada's asbestos came exclusively from Quebec's Eastern Townships, but operations have since been undertaken in Ontario, British Co1umbia, Newfoundland, the Yukon Territory and northern Québec. Canada is among the world's leading producers and exporters of the chrysotile variety of asbestos.

Existing plaque: On its own stand, to the right of a monument out front from the Timmins Museum National Exhibition Centre. 325 2nd Ave, Timmins, Ontario

Gold Mining

After the Cariboo and Klondike rushes, gold production in Canada entered a new era with the discovery of lode deposits in the Porcupine area in 1909. The mines in this district, notably the Hollinger which became one of the world's largest gold mines, along with those of the Kirkland Lake area, established in Canada as a leading producer of this metal. Their success was followed by other important finds at Rouyn in Quebec, in northern Manitoba, in British Columbia and at Yellowknife in the Northwest Territories.

Existing plaque: Labrador City, Newfoundland and Labrador

Iron Mining

Although there is evidence of Norse working of bog iron in Newfoundland in the 11th century, commercial extraction of iron in Canada began near Trois- Rivières in the 1730s. In the l9th century there were local workings in Upper Canada and Nova Scotia, but it was only in the 1890s that major deposits were discovered north of Lake Superior and here in western Labrador. Still, it took almost 50 years to overcome transportation problems; the first ore was shipped from Steep Rock in 1945, and from Knob Lake in 1954. By the mid-1970s the Labrador deposits accounted for 62% of Canada's iron production.

Existing plaque: Dynamic Earth 122 Big Nickel Road, Sudbury, Ontario

Nickel Mining

Canada is a leading producer of nickel and the mining of this metal has contributed significantly to the nation's economy. This industry is closely tied to the Sudbury Basin whose mines have produced the greater part of the metal in this country. Nickel, in association with other metals, was discovered here in 1883 and mining for copper began shortly thereafter; but it was not until the 1890's with the development of nickel- steel alloys that the ore was mined, primarily for its nickel. The Creighton mine which opened in 1900 became the largest nickel mine in the world.

Existing plaque: Parkette at Grandview Avenue and Jamieson Street, Cobalt, Ontario

Silver Mining

Silver has been an important mineral product for Canada's economy ever since the Cobalt boom which followed the discovery of rich veins of the metal near here in 1903. Although the production of the Cobalt silver mines began to decline in the 1920s, new sources were developed, principally in the lead and zinc mines of British Columbia and Ontario, which have maintained Canada's position in the world as a leading supplier of silver. The Cobalt boom was also important as a stimulus to future mining development in the Canadian Shield and as an influence on government mining policy.

Existing plaque: Eldorado Mine at Great Bear Lake, Port Radium, Northwest Territories

Uranium Industry

Gilbert LaBine's discovery of pitchblende on the shore of Great Bear Lake in May 1930 led to the opening of Canada's first uranium mine. Production, geared to the extraction of radium for medical science, was carried on from 1933 to 1940. The Port Radium mine, re-opened in 1942 under government auspices to produce uranium for the Allied atomic bomb programme, remained in operation until 1960. The development of nuclear powered generating stations in the post-war period has provided a stimulus to the Canadian uranium industry centred on the rich deposits of northern Saskatchewan and Ontario.
