Why this focus

The opportunity behind the headline.

A resource in the ground is not yet a reliable supply chain. Recovery, processing economics, demonstration capacity and customer qualification can prevent promising deposits and technologies from reaching market.

Government of Canada context

The Canadian Critical Minerals Strategy connects exploration, extraction, processing, manufacturing and recycling. It also emphasizes Indigenous reconciliation, environmental protection and resilient value chains.

Our proposed response

What CEMI and MICA would bring.

CEMI / Challenge and evidence

Translate operator and processor bottlenecks into quantified challenge briefs; compare technologies and structure independent demonstration evidence.

MICA / Route to market

Use MICA, where its mandate and capacity are confirmed, to connect Canadian SMEs with demonstration hosts, processing partners, buyers and commercialization expertise.

Proposed delivery roles only. Confirm MICA governance, active scope and available capacity for each engagement.

Where to start

Practical opportunities to explore.

Recovery and processing

Ore sorting, selective extraction and processing routes that improve recovery and product quality under real feed conditions.

Buyer qualification

Connect test results to product specifications, impurity limits and the evidence a downstream buyer needs.

Resilient supply chains

Identify domestic supplier gaps, second-source options and equipment or service businesses ready to scale.

Proposed first engagement

Resource-to-buyer demonstration

Practical output

A representative-feed test report, mass balance, product specification and buyer-reviewed techno-economic case.

Area-specific decision gate

Proceed only when recovery, product quality, environmental constraints and buyer acceptance criteria are met; an introduction is not an offtake agreement.

Evidence, not promises

How we would measure progress.

Agree baselines, targets, measurement methods and reporting permissions before work starts. These are proposed measures, not achieved results.

- 01Recovery (%) and product purity against the agreed feed baseline

- 02Processing cost ($/tonne) and energy (kWh/tonne) at the stated scale

- 03Buyer qualifications completed and paid deployments, reported separately

Responsible delivery

Boundaries that matter.

Verify the current federal mineral list and any program eligibility before making a claim. Do not imply guaranteed permits, financing, offtake or government endorsement.

For the first conversation, bring the problem or community-defined priority, your role, available evidence and the decision you need to make. Do not send confidential technical data, sensitive community information or Indigenous knowledge through the public inquiry form.

Connected services

Use the pathway that fits the need.

Challenge identification

Technology road maps and our Innovation-GPS systems analysis help uncover and validate operational challenges.

Solution matching

Connecting validated industry challenges with emerging technologies and the people who can deliver them.

Commercialization support

Identifying technical and commercial gaps, then building the partnerships and plans to bridge them.

Connected priorities

No challenge stands alone.

Indigenous partnerships and First Nations

Create space for First Nations, Inuit and Métis rights-holders and Indigenous businesses to shape innovation, participation and benefits on their own terms.

Clean energy and electrification

Turn mine electrification, energy efficiency and remote-power ideas into integrated, commercially credible deployment plans.

Water and nature

Help operators and participating communities evaluate water treatment, reuse and environmental monitoring with evidence suited to local conditions.

Circular mining and tailings

Evaluate recovery from tailings, recycling and reuse as complete technical, environmental and commercial systems.

Digital mining and responsible AI

Move from isolated digital pilots to secure, interoperable tools that operators can trust and maintain.

Workforce and mine safety

Treat skills, worker participation and safe operating practice as part of commercialization, not a task left until deployment.
