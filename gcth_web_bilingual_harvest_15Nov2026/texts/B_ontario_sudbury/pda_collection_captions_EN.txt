The Collection – Place des Arts (object captions)

White pine beams from Chicago

At the time of the Great Chicago Fire, the soon-to-be Sudbury District had busy lumber camps and their wood helped to rebuild the devastated city. Years later, Place des Arts features white pine salvaged from demolished buildings in Chicago.

Lockers from École secondaire Macdonald-Cartier

When Ontario decided to create publicly funded French-language high schools, one of the first in the province was built in Sudbury in 1969. It would soon earn a reputation as a centre of excellence for student theatre with the student company Les Draveurs.

Tin ceiling tiles from École Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague

Remarkably, Sudbury built this large Francophone elementary school despite Ontario’s Regulation XVII which prohibited French-language education at the time. Also known as the “l’école Centrale,” it welcomed thousands of students in its 85 years of service.

Copyright certificate for Lignes-signes

Following the TNO in 1971 and CANO in 1972, the founding of publishing house Prise de parole in 1973 further extended the “Nouvel-Ontario” movement. The first work published was “Lignes-Signes”, a collection of poems by four authors.

Bread pans from Canada Bread

Salvaged from the Canada Bread bakery building built in 1947 at 90 King Street, which housed the TNO in the 1990s, the bread pans were used in the “Canada Bread” production in 1991-92.

Stage decor resembling slag

Slag is the black molten rock left over from the smelting process in area mines. Made to resemble slag, these panels were first used for “Violette sur la terre,” and again for “Slague – L’histoire d’un mineur,” two plays produced by the TNO.

“Pi” – an installation by Lise Beaudry

“Pi” is a stack of 15,000 archival photos forming a 3.14 metre column. The number 3.14 abbreviates the infinite and irrational number pi, while 314 was the total years of existence of Place des Arts’ seven founding organizations in 2020.

Boards produced at Île-aux-Chênes

Île-aux-Chênes (Oak Island, a.k.a. Hardwood Island), on Lake Nipissing near Lavigne, was not only a nature-based summer camp for Francophone youth from across Ontario, but also a vibrant hub for arts and culture experiences, personal growth and community spirit.

Theatre lights from Sheridan Auditorium

Sudbury High School built a new 600-seat auditorium in 1951. Well suited for theatre and music productions, it served as a venue for many memorable Francophone events, including the first concerts hosted by La Slague in the early sixties.

Stained glass from Collège du Sacré-Cœur

Founded by the Jesuits in 1913 on the model of typically French-Canadian learning institutions, the college educated generations of community leaders. In 1957, it gave birth to Université de Sudbury, which federated with Laurentian University in 1960.
